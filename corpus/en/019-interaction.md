## Subject

The nineteenth LAB experiment was the first attempt to test
the project's central hypothesis **Interaction > Node** through
a designed experiment rather than post-hoc reconstruction.

Before 019, the hypothesis had only been tested indirectly:
in experiment 011, interaction was noticed as a side effect
of a baseline check. 019 was the first case where interaction
was designed in advance, with a pre-specified Target,
controls, and classification.

## What was tested

Target (R): **a priority rule between four conflicting
definitions of source reliability** — legal, scientific,
journalistic, and network-based.

The requirement for R was operational: not "depends on
context", but a switching rule of the form
"under condition X → definition [i], under condition Y →
definition [j]". Plus justification, boundaries of applicability,
and synthetic nature.

## How it was tested

- **Dialogue A↔B** (3 turns). Nodes know their response will
  be received by another node and that the other will respond.
- **C on transcript** — external observer, not participating,
  analyzes a completed transcript.
- **Isolation** (6 runs): Luna, Sakana, Grok in clean isolation, n=2.
- **Transcript-control** (2): node receives material
  without any feedback channel.
- **Anticipation** (1): node knows about parallel participation
  of another node, without receiving its text.
- **Qwen** — confounded condition, isolation only.

## What was obtained

**FACT.** R did not appear **in any run**: not in dialogue,
not in isolation, not in transcript, not in anticipation.
12 sessions of the main line + 2 Qwen — zero R.

**RESULT.** Level of 019 = **L1** on the METHOD v4 §7 scale.
Formally: the result equals the sum of node responses
without an interaction-driven transformation toward the Target.

**FACT.** All four nodes independently rejected the framing
"priority between definitions". Luna proposed 9 axes;
Sakana — a meta-criterion of convergence; Grok — a contextual
assessment; Qwen — a multidimensional matrix. Convergence
on rejection of the framing, not on R.

## What this means

**INTERPRETATION.** 019 is not a negative result for the
Interaction > Node hypothesis. It is a negative result
for the **specific Target R**.

**Formulation.** 019 did not answer whether interaction
produces a Target at all. It showed that the chosen Target
does not arise in any tested configuration.

**OPEN.** Dominant hypothesis from external review:
Target/material mismatch. The material (four incommensurable
frameworks) systematically pushes nodes toward rejecting
the framing. Alternative: A1 was too strict.
Not distinguishable within 019.

## What 019 contributed methodologically

**RESULT.** Three findings independent of the Target:

1. **Transcript-control works as a content-transfer control.**
   Sakana and Grok on transcript reproduced the structure
   of the supplied material almost verbatim.
2. **Dialogue and transcript are distinguishable.**
   In dialogue, nodes produce their own moves. In transcript,
   they reproduce the material.
3. **Anticipation is a working control.** Knowledge of
   parallel participation without text did not change
   Luna's response.

## Open questions

**OPEN.** Not distinguishable within 019:

- Whether the Target was poorly chosen, A1 was too strict,
  or interaction does not produce R in this configuration.
- Dialogue form: are three turns sufficient or too few.
- A1 as criterion: whether to revise for 020.

**OPEN.** What comes next:

- New Target for 020, specifically reachable in principle
  (preflight in addendum §12).
- Testing transcript-control on a **positive** Target case:
  Target present in interaction, absent in isolation
  and transcript-control.

## What 019 does not mean

- It does not mean interaction cannot produce new structures.
- It does not mean the addendum procedure fails.
  The addendum worked end-to-end as a methodological framework.
- It does not mean the Interaction > Node hypothesis is weakened.
  It was not tested — there was nothing to test.
